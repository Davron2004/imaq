## Description (250)

Imaq lets families in truck-served Nunavik villages ask for water by scanning a QR code on their door, gives drivers an offline, glove-friendly log with voice notes, and gives the water office one screen. No more laps to spot door lights.

## How it addresses the problem (700)

Today a truck drives the whole village to spot lit doors, goes back to fill up, then drives the village again to deliver. Every request costs a scouting lap, and that hurts most when two of three trucks are broken. With Imaq, residents tap Need water soon, Out of water, Emergency or Sewage full over home Wi-Fi, so drivers know the litres and truckloads before they fill. The driver app works fully offline and syncs at the plant. Stops, couldn't-deliver reasons, truck checks and breakdowns become records. The door light keeps working: if Imaq is down, the village works exactly as it does today.

## Social impact (700)

In a studied Nunavik village, a third of households ran short of water in a single week. Imaq cuts wasted driving so the same trucks reach more homes, and emergencies go first. It creates the first delivery record these villages have: which homes got water between two dates, so a bad E. coli result from Montreal becomes an advisory for exactly those homes. Truck-down history and weekly numbers become evidence for the pipeline the mayor is already seeking funding for. It costs $0 to run, needs no admin, collects no names or medical details, and is built for elders, gloves and glare.

## Unique value (700)

Kiujik and GovTec handle requests and dispatch. Imaq adds what they don't: it removes the scouting lap in light-on-door villages, it records drivers' field knowledge by voice in any language (AI turns a rambling note into a structured entry the driver confirms, and the original audio is kept), and it traces a bad lab result to the exact homes served. AI never decides who gets water or predicts repairs; every rule is plain logic staff can check by hand. It's open source (MIT), multi-village from day one, runs on free tiers, and a side-by-side simulation shows the effect under the breakdowns and blizzards these villages live through.
